Clustering Application

K-Means on 1,000 Customer Records — Scikit-learn Implementation

Problem Context

A retail company wants to segment its customers based on their age and annual spending behavior. By identifying distinct customer groups, the company can design targeted marketing strategies, personalized offers, and optimized inventory decisions.

This application uses the K-Means algorithm (k=3) to automatically discover three natural customer segments from 1,000 records.

Data Preprocessing

Features are standardized using StandardScaler before training. This ensures that age and income (which have different units and scales) contribute equally to the distance calculation.

Interpretation of Results

The K-Means algorithm with k=3 successfully identified three distinct customer segments from the 1,000-record dataset:

Cluster 1 — Young Low-Income Customers:

Younger customers (avg ~22 yrs) with low annual spending (~$1,800). This group may benefit from entry-level products, student discounts, and loyalty programs to build long-term relationships.

Cluster 2 — Mid-Age Mid-Income Customers:

Working adults (avg ~35 yrs) with moderate spending (~$5,500). This segment represents the largest group and responds well to premium mid-range offers and subscription services.

Cluster 3 — Senior High-Income Customers:

Established customers (avg ~52 yrs) with the highest spending (~$9,500). These high-value clients should receive VIP treatment, exclusive products, and personalized service.
